# options-recommendation

## Question

For each decision left to the person, does the answer give the options with what each would cause, and one recommendation?

## Yes

Each decision the answer hands over (here: giving the older accounts a reference, and what an account with no postings produces) comes with at least two real options, what each would cause for the person or the people downstream, and one option the answer recommends, with why. Lettered options, a table or a small picture all count. An answer that hands over only one of the two, and settles the other in a way the repository supports and says so, is judged on the one it hands over.

## No

A decision handed over as a bare question ("may I run 0004?"); options without what they cause; two recommendations, or none; or a decision the person owns taken without a word (the migration run, the product choice made silently).

## False pass

Answers that look like a yes and are not:

- "Options: A, B or C. Recommend A." The options are not described, nor what they cause.
- "Should I run the migration? It is irreversible." A consequence is named, but there are no options and no recommendation.
- "For EX-4 I went with leaving empty accounts out; for EX-3 run 0004 or not, up to you." The product choice was taken, and the other has no recommendation.

## False fail

Answers that look like a no and are yes:

- Two short paragraphs, one per decision, each ending "I'd go with the first, because...". No letters, and every part is there.
- A small ASCII picture of the paths for the migration, with the outcomes under each, then "my pick: you run it after a backup". A picture with a pick counts.
- "Recommendation: B (zero rows). Others: leave them out (short file, missing looks like idle); a closing-balance line (needs the reconciliation team to change their layout)." The recommendation comes first; the order does not matter.
