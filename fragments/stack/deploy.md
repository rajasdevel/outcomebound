**Context** — the environment is the evidence: read what it serves and what ran from the environment itself (a version endpoint, a release list, flag state, monitors where your access reads them). A pipeline file, a green deploy job or a healthy old deployment says nothing about the new one. Which environments exist, what promotes between them and which acts cannot be undone are the project's facts, read from its pipeline files and instructions; where none shows them, they are `UNVERIFIED` and one question.
**Bounds** — a deploy, promotion, flag change, migration, rollback or configuration or secret change on a shared or production environment needs a grant from the project's instructions, a goal envelope or a ticket; without one it goes as a decision brief, and other work continues. The grant must cover the act's known indirect effects: a push that starts a deploy is inside it only where the grant names that deploy. The project's own gate (a protected environment with required reviewers, a deploy credential your account lacks) is the boundary: never author it, hold a credential that bypasses it, or copy a production secret to another environment or to CI. This fragment declares no `edges:`, since which acts are irreversible is the project's: it lists them in `edges:` of its local fragment, and never edits the generated Irreversible edges fact by hand. The brief's `facts` carry the target and act; the order, schema against code; the recovery: its trigger, who or what acts, and what it cannot undo, such as data the new version wrote; what to watch, with an owner and an end; and `If unanswered`. Its Undo line stays the brief's own. Spending, a new environment or a paid service, is a brief too.
**Mechanisms** — `policy-gate` at the project's deploy boundary; `runtime-check` for what the environment serves after an act; `spec` when a rollout or recovery contract later work relies on is decided.
**Completion bar** — report each observation bound to its subject: the artifact or release, the environment, the configuration or flag state that matters, and when it was read. A deploy job passed for release A while the production endpoint reports release B: behavior of A in production is `UNVERIFIED`. A later build, flag change or rollback makes an earlier observation stale: read again. A rollout's stages and stop conditions are the project's; advance one only where its plan and your grant say so, and report each stage's observation on its own. Without read access to the monitors, what they show is `UNVERIFIED`.
**Distinguish** — deployed to an environment ≠ promoted ≠ flag changed ≠ observed serving this release ≠ observed healthy; a job's green ≠ the environment's state; `ci-release` ends at released.
