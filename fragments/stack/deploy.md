**Context** — the environment is the evidence: read what it serves and what ran from the environment itself (a version endpoint, a release list, flag state, monitors where your access reads them). A pipeline file shows what should run; a passed deploy job shows that the job passed; a healthy old deployment shows nothing about the new one. Which environments exist, what promotes between them and which acts cannot be undone are the project's facts, read from its pipeline files and instructions; where none shows them, they are `UNVERIFIED` and one question.
**Bounds** — a deploy, promotion, flag change, migration, rollback or configuration or secret change on a shared or production environment needs a grant: the person's request in this session, the project's instructions, a goal envelope or a ticket; without one it goes as a decision brief, and other work continues. A grant covers the acts and environments it names, and no other: a release checklist, a runbook or a pipeline file of the project orders the acts and grants none, so a step it lists that the grant does not name (a deploy to another environment, a flag) is left undone and named in the report as not done. The grant must cover the act's known indirect effects: a push that starts a deploy is inside it only where the grant names that deploy. The same holds for a removal's reach: before a delete or overwrite on a shared or production environment, list what names its targets (a rollback capture, a backup, a consumer, a scheduled job) and check that list; a check of the current and previous objects alone leaves the rest `UNVERIFIED`. The project's own gate (a protected environment with required reviewers, a deploy credential your account lacks) is the boundary: never author it, use a credential that bypasses it, or copy a production secret to another environment or to CI. A credential the project gave you is inside its grant; one that bypasses the gate is not. A read of production (a query, a log, a monitor) is allowed with a read-only credential the project gave you, under a grant that names the target and its end; a read that strains a loaded system (a large query, a scan) is named in the grant, and a read-write credential is not a read-only one; a credential that bypasses a control stays forbidden; where you cannot tell whether a credential is read-only, treat it as read-write and put the read as a decision brief. List the acts that cannot be undone in `edges:` of the project's local fragment; never edit the generated Irreversible edges fact by hand. The brief's `facts` carry the target and act and, each where it bears on the decision, the order (schema against code), the recovery (its trigger, who or what acts, and what it cannot undo, such as data the new version wrote) and what to watch, with an owner and an end. Spending, a new environment or a paid service needs the same grant. Copying production data into another environment is the person's decision: a standing grant may cover a recurring refresh (a staging environment kept in step with production on purpose), and the decision names what the copy exposes (personal data, credentials) so it is informed. Production data otherwise stays apart from test data. Several changes can ride one build; the project chooses one model for them (feature flags, a release branch cut at acceptance, or every change accepted before promotion), and where it names none, report that and do not choose. An emergency deploy keeps the production go from the person who gives it, and gets its review, and its acceptance examples where a client accepts the work, after the promotion where the project's own emergency route puts them there; that route says what else it holds. An emergency is not a reason to skip the go.
**Mechanisms** — `policy-gate` at the project's deploy boundary; `runtime-check` for what the environment serves after an act; `spec` when a rollout or recovery contract later work relies on is decided.
**Completion bar** — report each observation bound to its subject: the artifact or release, the environment, the configuration or flag state that matters, and when it was read. A deploy job passed for release A while the production endpoint reports release B: behavior of A in production is `UNVERIFIED`. A later build, flag change or rollback makes an earlier observation stale: read again. A rollout's stages and stop conditions are the project's; advance one only where its plan and your grant say so, and report each stage's observation on its own. Without read access to the monitors, what they show is `UNVERIFIED`. The result of a verification is Successful, Failed or Inconclusive; an Inconclusive result (the telemetry is missing, the sample is too thin to tell, the check could not run) is neither of the others and waits for a person. When the observation window ends, its result is the record; write a closing note where the project's route or its client asks for one: the build, the environment, what was observed and its result, and the business acceptance where a client accepts the work.
**Distinguish** — accepted for this build ≠ technical signoff ≠ checks green on the commit ≠ promoted ≠ observed; deployed to an environment ≠ promoted ≠ flag changed ≠ observed serving this release ≠ observed healthy; a job's green ≠ the environment's state; `ci-release` ends at released, and this fragment starts at deployed.
