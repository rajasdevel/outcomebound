---
id: client
family: setup
applies: projects where a client or a regulator accepts the work
condition: when preparing, running or recording an acceptance, promoting a build a client or regulator accepts, or recording who reviewed or approved a change
detect: []
version: 1
---
**Context** — this applies to the streams the project names for it in its `local` fragment's `Streams —` lines; elsewhere it adds nothing. An acceptance covers one build and is given by one named person. Your own pre-run of the acceptance scripts is evidence for that person, not the acceptance. A tracker event under a shared login proves nothing about who acted, so the record carries the person's own words (the message, the signed note or the quoted line, with where it was given). Who accepts, and the project's own gates, are lines of the project's `local` fragment (`Gates —`); where none names them, they are `UNVERIFIED` and one question. The `run-acceptance` skill carries the work of preparing, pre-running and recording an acceptance, and the `sync-tracker` skill the reading of a tracker that is not the ticket store.
**Bounds** — only an accepted build is promoted; a later build voids the acceptance for every change it touches, and the unchanged parts keep it only where the record shows what the later build left alone. A defect found in acceptance becomes a ticket, not a note on the acceptance. A review counts as a second party's only when a person other than the author gives it on their own account; under a shared login, or when the author is the only reviewer, the record names that loss and the stronger check used instead (a check that fails on the defect, or a run in the environment that holds the risk). A person decides only at an irreversible edge, the outside contract or an unsettled intent: an accept, a reject, the promotion of an accepted build, a change to what the contract says. A step inside the unit of work is not a gate, and a gate the project names in its `Gates —` lines is the person's to pass, never one you author or stand in for. A gate the project names without an owner is reported as a gap before the work reaches it, and you do not fill it. Writing to the client's tracker, sending the client a message or recording an acceptance in their name is the person's, as a decision brief.
**Mechanisms** — `policy-gate` at the project's own promotion boundary, not one you author; `review` when a change alters what the client accepted; `spec` when the contract or an acceptance example later work relies on is decided.
**Completion bar** — the acceptance record names the build (a digest, a tag or a commit), the person who accepted it, their words and the date, and lists the examples run and each result. Promotion of the accepted build reads `UNVERIFIED` until the project has a release identity: a digest, a tag or a commit that production runs. A pre-run that passed shows the scripts passed on that build; it settles nothing about the client's acceptance.
**Distinguish** — pre-run passed ≠ accepted; accepted for this build ≠ accepted for a later build; a tracker event ≠ the person's word; a reviewer's approval ≠ a second party's review ≠ the author's own check; accepted ≠ promoted.
